\hypertarget{the-overlap-illusion}{%
\section{The overlap illusion}\label{the-overlap-illusion}}

The rolling analysis re-estimates the ranking on a window of length
$W=120$ months advanced by a step of $s=12$ months. Two windows separated
by $k$ steps share their data except for the $ks$ months at each end, so
their overlap fraction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch10-overlap}
\text{overlap}(k) = \frac{\max(0,\,W-ks)}{W} = \max\!\Bigl(0,\,1-\frac{ks}{W}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Adjacent windows ($k=1$) therefore share $108/120=90\%$ of their months.
A high correlation between two such rankings is largely \emph{mechanical}:
they are computed from almost the same data, so agreement measures shared
inputs, not economic persistence (\S\ref{rq-difficulties}). Genuine
structural persistence can only be read from \emph{non-overlapping}
comparisons, which require $ks\ge W$, i.e.\ $k\ge W/s=10$ steps, a
separation of $120$ months. At that horizon the two ten-year windows are
disjoint and the correlation is interpretable.

\hypertarget{persistence-metrics}{%
\section{Persistence metrics}\label{persistence-metrics}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Spearman rank correlation:} agreement in order, invariant to
  monotone score transformations.
\item
  \textbf{Pearson score correlation:} linear agreement in estimated
  posterior scores.
\item
  \textbf{Mean absolute rank change:} average number of positions moved.
\item
  \textbf{Transition matrix:} probability of moving from one quintile to
  another.
\item
  \textbf{Jaccard overlap:} intersection divided by union for top/bottom
  membership sets.
\end{itemize}

Mobility is summarised by a \emph{transition matrix} over the five score
quintiles. With $q_{it}$ the quintile of portfolio $i$ at time $t$, the
entry
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch10-transition}
P_{jk} = \Pr\bigl(q_{i,t+h}=k \mid q_{it}=j\bigr),
\qquad \sum_{k=1}^{5} P_{jk}=1,
\end{equation}
is the probability that a portfolio in quintile $j$ moves to quintile $k$
over horizon $h$; each row is a probability distribution. The natural
null is \emph{uniform mobility}: if next-period quintile were independent
of the current one, every $P_{jk}$ would be $1/5=20\%$, so a stay
probability $P_{jj}$ near $20\%$ signals essentially no persistence, while
$P_{jj}=1$ would mean a frozen hierarchy. At the disjoint 120-month
horizon the mean same-quintile probability is about $25\%$ --- only
modestly above the $20\%$ uniform benchmark and far below a frozen
ordering. Combined with the weak non-overlapping rank correlation, this
warns strongly against treating the point ordering as a stable league
table; it is the estimation counterpart of the wide bootstrap rank
intervals of Chapter~9.
