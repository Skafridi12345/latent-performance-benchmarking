\hypertarget{four-different-validation-questions}{%
\section{Four different validation
questions}\label{four-different-validation-questions}}

\begin{longtable}[]{@{}p{0.20\textwidth}p{0.34\textwidth}p{0.36\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule\noalign{}
Validation & Question & Evidence \\
\midrule\noalign{}
\endhead
\bottomrule\noalign{}
\endlastfoot
Integrity & Is the local analytical panel internally valid? & Keys,
nulls, calendar, balance, factor consistency \\
Numerical replication & Does an independent implementation reproduce
calculations? & statsmodels coefficient/HAC match \\
Source reconciliation & Do local bytes match an identifiable official
release? & Hashes, archives, overlapping-cell comparison \\
Literature anchor & Are broad patterns coherent with established
evidence? & 5 \ensuremath{\times } 5 loadings/returns; historical sample checks \\
\end{longtable}

\textbf{Do not collapse these into ``validated.''} A calculation can
reproduce perfectly on a locally corrupted input. Conversely, a fixed
historical vintage can be internally valid even when it differs from a
later revised official snapshot.

\hypertarget{known-result-validation-without-result-fitting}{%
\section{Known-result validation without result
fitting}\label{known-result-validation-without-result-fitting}}

Check broad expectations --- such as the HML loading increasing toward high
book-to-market and the SMB exposure changing across size groups (the
Frisch--Waugh reading of \S\ref{alpha-sampling}) --- but treat them as
diagnostics. Unexpected patterns trigger investigation; they must not be
overwritten or tuned away, or the ``check'' would merely confirm a
result it was fitted to.

\begin{verbatim}
comparison = local.merge(official, on=["date","portfolio"], suffixes=("_local","_official"))
comparison["abs_diff"] = (
    comparison["return_local"] - comparison["return_official"]
).abs()
summary = {
    "max_abs_diff": comparison["abs_diff"].max(),
    "date_of_max": comparison.loc[comparison["abs_diff"].idxmax(), "date"],
    "n_above_tol": int((comparison["abs_diff"] > tolerance).sum()),
}
\end{verbatim}

\textbf{Paper result.} The four layers resolve differently, which is
exactly why they must be kept separate.
\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{Integrity} passes: the balanced panel of 29{,}825 portfolio-months
  has no duplicate keys, null required fields, calendar gaps or
  within-month factor disagreement (Chapter~4).
\item
  \emph{Numerical replication} passes: all 25 portfolios' OLS coefficients
  and HAC standard errors reproduce with \texttt{statsmodels} to a
  tolerance of $10^{-12}$ (Chapter~6).
\item
  \emph{Literature anchor} passes: the $5\times5$ loading and return
  patterns are economically coherent, and the July 1963--December 1991
  historical anchor rejects joint zero alpha under HAC/Wald while GRS does
  not (Chapter~13).
\item
  \emph{Source reconciliation} does \emph{not} pass: the fixed local inputs
  differ from the current official snapshot by up to $1.4167$ percentage
  points (returns) and $0.34$ percentage points (factors) over their
  overlap. Because the local acquisition vintage is unverified, this is
  classified \textsc{not\_comparable}, not \textsc{fail} --- a visible,
  non-blocking discrepancy whose cause (a plausible official revision)
  cannot be uniquely attributed without the exact archived release.
\end{itemize}
Collapsing these into a single ``validated'' badge would hide the one layer
that is genuinely open; reporting them separately is the honest summary.
