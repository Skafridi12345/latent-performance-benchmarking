\hypertarget{the-winners-curse}{%
\section{The winner\textquotesingle s curse}\label{the-winners-curse}}

When the largest estimate is selected from 25 noisy estimates, it is
likely to be unusually high partly because of noise: even if every true
alpha were equal, the observed maximum would exceed the truth by about
$\sigma\sqrt{2\ln N}$ (\S\ref{rq-difficulties}). Partial pooling corrects
this by shrinking estimates toward a cross-sectional centre, with the amount
of shrinkage set by sampling uncertainty and the estimated dispersion of the
true alphas; the James--Stein dominance result (\S\ref{prelim-eb})
guarantees this reduces risk in dimension $N\ge3$. The applied hierarchy
uses the joint HAC covariance of Chapter~6 as the sampling model:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-hierarchy}
\hat\alpha\mid\alpha \sim N(\alpha,\hat V_{\mathrm{HAC}}),
\qquad \alpha \sim N(\mu\mathbf{1},\tau^2 I),
\end{equation}
with posterior mean and covariance
\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}(\alpha\mid\text{data})
&= \mu\mathbf{1} + \tau^2(\hat V_{\mathrm{HAC}}+\tau^2 I)^{-1}(\hat\alpha-\mu\mathbf{1}),
\label{eq:ch8-postmean}\\
\operatorname{Var}(\alpha\mid\text{data})
&= \tau^2 I - \tau^4(\hat V_{\mathrm{HAC}}+\tau^2 I)^{-1}.
\label{eq:ch8-postvar}
\end{align}
With a diagonal $V$, each portfolio shrinks independently. With the full
$V$, a portfolio's posterior update also reflects how its alpha-estimation
error co-moves with the others --- the multivariate feature that a
portfolio-by-portfolio shrinkage would miss.

\hypertarget{deriving-the-posterior-by-completing-the-square}{%
\section{Deriving the posterior by completing the
square}\label{deriving-the-posterior-by-completing-the-square}}

Write $a=\hat\alpha$ and let $\mathbf{1}$ denote the $N$-vector of ones.
Ignoring constants that do not depend on $\alpha$, Bayes' rule
(\S\ref{prelim-eb}) gives the log posterior
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-logpost}
\log p(\alpha\mid a) = -\tfrac12(a-\alpha)^{\top}V^{-1}(a-\alpha)
 - \tfrac12(\alpha-\mu\mathbf{1})^{\top}(\tau^{-2}I)(\alpha-\mu\mathbf{1}) + C.
\end{equation}
Collecting the quadratic and linear terms in $\alpha$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-precision}
\log p(\alpha\mid a) = -\tfrac12\alpha^{\top}P\alpha + \alpha^{\top}b + C',
\qquad P = V^{-1}+\tau^{-2}I,\quad b = V^{-1}a+\tau^{-2}\mu\mathbf{1}.
\end{equation}

Completing the square with $C_{\mathrm{post}}=P^{-1}$ and
$m_{\mathrm{post}}=C_{\mathrm{post}}\,b$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-complete}
-\tfrac12\alpha^{\top}P\alpha+\alpha^{\top}b
= -\tfrac12(\alpha-m_{\mathrm{post}})^{\top}P(\alpha-m_{\mathrm{post}})
 + \tfrac12 m_{\mathrm{post}}^{\top}P\,m_{\mathrm{post}},
\end{equation}
so $\alpha\mid a\sim N(m_{\mathrm{post}},C_{\mathrm{post}})$ with precision-form
solution $C_{\mathrm{post}}=(V^{-1}+\tau^{-2}I)^{-1}$ and
$m_{\mathrm{post}}=C_{\mathrm{post}}(V^{-1}a+\tau^{-2}\mu\mathbf{1})$.

\textbf{From the precision form to the update form.} The precision form
inverts $V$, which is inconvenient and numerically poor. The key algebraic
fact is that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-factor}
P = V^{-1}+\tau^{-2}I = \tau^{-2}V^{-1}(V+\tau^2 I)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P^{-1} = \tau^2 (V+\tau^2 I)^{-1} V .
\end{equation}
Substituting into $C_{\mathrm{post}}=P^{-1}$ and simplifying (equivalently,
the Woodbury identity \eqref{eq:eb-woodbury}) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-Cpost}
C_{\mathrm{post}} = (V^{-1}+\tau^{-2}I)^{-1} = \tau^2 I - \tau^4 (V+\tau^2 I)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
For the mean, split $a=(a-\mu\mathbf{1})+\mu\mathbf{1}$ so that
$b=V^{-1}(a-\mu\mathbf{1})+P\mu\mathbf{1}$; then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-mpost-deriv}
m_{\mathrm{post}} = P^{-1}b
= \mu\mathbf{1} + P^{-1}V^{-1}(a-\mu\mathbf{1})
= \mu\mathbf{1} + \tau^2(V+\tau^2 I)^{-1}(a-\mu\mathbf{1}),
\end{equation}
the last step using $P^{-1}V^{-1}=\tau^2(V+\tau^2 I)^{-1}$ from
\eqref{eq:ch8-factor}. Defining the \emph{shrinkage matrix}
$B=\tau^2(V+\tau^2 I)^{-1}$, the update reads
$m_{\mathrm{post}}=\mu\mathbf{1}+B(a-\mu\mathbf{1})$: each raw alpha is pulled
toward the common centre $\mu$ by the matrix $B$, whose diagonal entries are
the per-portfolio shrinkage weights reported in the results.

\textbf{Uncertainty in the estimated common mean.} Equations
\eqref{eq:ch8-Cpost}--\eqref{eq:ch8-mpost-deriv} condition on a fixed $\mu$,
but $\mu$ is itself profiled from the data (next section). Because the
posterior mean loads on $\mu$ through $(I-B)\mathbf{1}$, the reported
covariance adds a first-order term for that estimation uncertainty,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-muterm}
\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\alpha\mid\text{data})
= C_{\mathrm{post}} + \operatorname{Var}(\hat\mu)\,(I-B)\mathbf{1}\,\mathbf{1}^{\top}(I-B)^{\top},
\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\hat\mu)=\frac{1}{\mathbf{1}^{\top}S(\tau)^{-1}\mathbf{1}} .
\end{equation}
Uncertainty in $\tau$ is \emph{not} integrated, and $V$ is treated as known
during the conditional posterior, so the intervals are model-based
approximations rather than exact.

\textbf{Scalar special case.} If $V$ is diagonal with entries $s_i^2$, then
$B$ is diagonal and the update reduces to the familiar scalar weighted
average
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-scalar}
\mathbb{E}(\alpha_i\mid a_i) = w_i a_i + (1-w_i)\mu,
\qquad w_i = \frac{\tau^2}{\tau^2+s_i^2}.
\end{equation}
For example, with $\mu=-26$, $\tau=94$, $a_i=250$ and $s_i=150$ bps/year,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-example}
w_i = \frac{94^2}{94^2+150^2} = 0.282,
\qquad
\mathbb{E}(\alpha_i\mid a_i) = 0.282(250)+0.718(-26) = 51.8\ \text{bps/year}.
\end{equation}
The estimate shrinks sharply because its sampling uncertainty exceeds the
cross-sectional prior dispersion. In the full multivariate calculation
there is no independent scalar weight per portfolio: the off-diagonal
entries of $V$ (hence of $B$) couple all 25 updates.

\hypertarget{estimating-ux3bc-and-ux3c4-by-marginal-likelihood}{%
\section{Estimating \ensuremath{\mu } and \ensuremath{\tau } by marginal
likelihood}\label{estimating-ux3bc-and-ux3c4-by-marginal-likelihood}}

The prior parameters are not known; empirical Bayes estimates them from the
same cross-section by maximising the \emph{marginal} likelihood
(\S\ref{prelim-eb}). Integrating $\alpha$ out of \eqref{eq:ch8-hierarchy}
gives the marginal model $\hat\alpha\sim N(\mu\mathbf{1},\,V+\tau^2 I)$.
Profile marginal maximum likelihood searches over $\tau\ge0$, solves for
$\mu$ conditional on $\tau$, and selects the pair that maximises the
likelihood. Let $S(\tau)=V+\tau^2 I$. The marginal log-likelihood is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-marglik}
\ell(\mu,\tau) = -\tfrac12\bigl[N\log(2\pi)+\log\lvert S(\tau)\rvert
 + (a-\mu\mathbf{1})^{\top}S(\tau)^{-1}(a-\mu\mathbf{1})\bigr].
\end{equation}
For fixed $\tau$ it is quadratic in $\mu$; differentiating,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-dmu}
\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\mu} = \mathbf{1}^{\top}S(\tau)^{-1}(a-\mu\mathbf{1}),
\end{equation}
and setting this to zero yields the generalised-least-squares profile
estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch8-mu}
\hat\mu(\tau) = \frac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}S(\tau)^{-1}a}{\mathbf{1}^{\top}S(\tau)^{-1}\mathbf{1}} .
\end{equation}
Substituting $\hat\mu(\tau)$ back into $\ell$ leaves a one-dimensional
profile in $\tau\ge0$, maximised numerically. Optimising over $\log\tau$
enforces positivity, but the $\tau=0$ boundary must be evaluated
explicitly rather than excluded by the transformation, since a genuinely
zero cross-sectional dispersion (complete pooling) is admissible.

\begin{verbatim}
def profiled_mu(alpha_hat, V, tau2):
    S = V + tau2 * np.eye(len(alpha_hat))
    one = np.ones(len(alpha_hat))
    Sinv_one = np.linalg.solve(S, one)
    return (Sinv_one @ alpha_hat) / (Sinv_one @ one)

def neg_profile_loglik(log_tau, alpha_hat, V):
    tau2 = np.exp(2 * log_tau)
    S = V + tau2 * np.eye(len(alpha_hat))
    mu = profiled_mu(alpha_hat, V, tau2)
    d = alpha_hat - mu
    sign, logdet = np.linalg.slogdet(S)
    if sign <= 0:
        return np.inf
    return 0.5 * (logdet + d @ np.linalg.solve(S, d))
\end{verbatim}
